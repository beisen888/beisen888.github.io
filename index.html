  • 8 pages

The question
How do you sell documentation software to hospice leaders whose biggest problem is staffing, not software?
The whitepaper
It meets leaders where the pain is. MGMT 4920, team of four, Sept 2026

Screen for objects, not behavior

We recommended the TSA retire SPOT, its behavior-detection program, and move that budget into scanners that find the dangerous object itself.

My role: I presented why our solution works, plus the implementation plan and the financials behind it.

  • 0.52% of SPOT referrals arrested
  • $200M a year redirected
  • 140–200 upgraded lanes a year

View the full deck (PDF)

The question
How can TSA stop dangerous objects and credible threats from getting on planes, instead of trying to predict which passengers might be dangerous?
Why SPOT fails
Only 0.52% of passengers referred through SPOT were arrested, and none of those arrests involved a threat to air travel. Each referral meant a 13-minute full search, and the program costs about $200M a year.
What we recommended
Phase out SPOT and redirect its budget into CT scanners for carry-ons and millimeter-wave body scanners, with a scanner alarm as the only trigger for secondary screening, the same for everyone. At $1.0–1.4M per lane, the same $200M buys 140–200 upgraded lanes a year: a pilot at 8–10 airports first, nationwide by 2035.
Proof it works
Heathrow chose the same body scanner after head-to-head testing, and it doubled screening throughput compared with the scanners it replaced.

Business Capstone, team of four, fall 2026

Connected decentralization at Qualico

How a fast-growing, decentralized homebuilder and real estate company can coordinate better without losing the entrepreneurial culture that made it successful.

My role: I owned the problem statement, the problem validation, and the solution feasibility analysis.

  • 3 paths compared
  • 4 shared pillars
  • 24 month rollout
The question
How can Qualico improve coordination and collaboration without sacrificing the autonomy and entrepreneurial culture behind its competitive advantage?
The diagnosis
Qualico’s business units share one value chain but compete with each other for capital. Leadership can’t see who is at capacity, every unit pitches for capital its own way, and best practices only move at a twice-a-year forum. Its structure doesn’t match its strategy.
What we recommended
Connected decentralization. Corporate centralizes four things: data and KPIs, a standard capital scorecard, a talent register, and a best-practice hub. Business units keep product, pricing, customers, suppliers, hiring, land, and day-to-day execution. We compared it against full centralization and scaling up the status quo, and it was the only option that balanced visibility with autonomy.

Strategic analysis, class project

Where Amerisafe wins

An industry analysis of Amerisafe, a workers’ compensation insurer, to find what threatens its profits and what protects them.

My role: I built the PESTEL and Five Forces analysis and the final deck, revising both after professor feedback.

The question
What forces shape workers’ comp insurance, and where does Amerisafe have an edge?
What we found
A PESTEL scan surfaced OSHA deregulation, employment levels, an aging and less experienced workforce, AI and wearables, climate exposure, and eroding exclusive-remedy protections. The verdict: rivalry is the biggest threat to profits, and specialization is Amerisafe’s core advantage.
